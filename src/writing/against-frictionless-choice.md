---
title: Against Frictionless Choice
date: 2025-09-08
excerpt: On the moral value of difficulty, and what smooth interfaces take from us.
---

The design ideal of the last two decades has been the removal of friction: fewer steps, less
hesitation, no gap between wanting and having. It is a good ideal for most things, and a
quietly corrosive one for a few.

Difficulty is not merely a cost. A pause is where a preference gets examined, where a
first impulse is allowed to be revised, where the person we are at three in the morning is
kept at some distance from the person who has to live with the result.

Removing friction from a decision does not make the decision easier. It makes it smaller —
and a life composed of very small decisions is not obviously one anybody chose.

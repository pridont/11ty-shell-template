---
title: Notes on Habit and Character
date: 2025-06-17
excerpt: How small repeated acts quietly author the person we become.
---

Aristotle's claim that we become just by doing just acts has always sounded either trivial or
circular. It is neither. It is a claim about how repetition changes perception — about the
way an act performed often enough stops being a choice and becomes a way of seeing.

The mechanism is unglamorous. Do a thing enough times and it costs less; costing less, it is
reached for sooner; reached for sooner, it starts to arrive before deliberation does. What
began as an action ends as a disposition.

This cuts both ways, which is the part worth sitting with. The same process that builds a
virtue builds its opposite, at the same rate, out of the same materials — and neither
announces itself while it is happening.

---
title: What We Owe to Strangers
date: 2026-01-20
excerpt: Reconsidering proximity, obligation, and the ethics of the near and the far.
---

Distance has always done quiet work in our moral thinking. The person in front of us makes a
claim that the person across the world does not, and most ethical traditions have been
willing to honour that difference without ever quite justifying it.

The standard move is to argue the difference away: if suffering is suffering, proximity is
morally arbitrary. I think that argument is right about the arithmetic and wrong about the
phenomenology. Proximity is not a reason, but it is a condition — it is what makes a claim
visible to us in the first place.

What follows is not that we owe less to strangers, but that we owe them something we rarely
name: the effort of noticing at all, unaided by the accident of nearness.
